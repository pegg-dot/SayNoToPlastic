## 3. Homepage: exact narrative and visual requirements

### Hero

- Use the core premise: **“Plastic is not just an environmental problem.”**
- Keep the larger book line: **“The most dangerous pollutant is the one already inside us.”**
- Remove “From Environmental Crisis to Human Biology,” “Story of Plastics Engine,” and related decorative explanatory boxes.
- Introduce the project as physician-led without making the hero a wall of medical caveats.
- Add a small, clear **scroll prompt** for visitors who may not understand the anatomy interaction.
- Include an entry point for Dr. Haddad’s short future introductory video: a small modal or panel, not a giant competing hero block. The video should introduce him, the mission, what microplastics are, where they come from, and why the topic matters.
- The Home CTA must take visitors to a working book purchase path. It cannot be a dead visual control.

### Anatomy story: required sequence

| Chapter | Structure shown | Reader-facing finding | Interaction acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Whole body / circulation | Full human body with circulatory context. | Plastic has been detected in human blood. | Visitor understands this is the starting full-body view. |
| 2. Brain | Recognizable brain with a clear camera transition. | Use the Nature Medicine tissue finding with direct study link. | The brain is visible, not merely implied by a pan across an image. |
| 3. Heart and arteries | Actual heart plus coronary circulation / vascular context. | Preserve the 2024 carotid-plaque evidence as a distinct study and include the 2025 European Heart Journal acute-heart-attack coronary-blood result. | Do not claim the colloquial “three times higher” headline unless the displayed source supports the exact calculation. |
| 4. Pregnancy and placenta | Clearly female torso, uterus, fetus, umbilical connection, and placenta. | Explain the placental finding in normal language, including maternal and fetal-facing context. | This must be one coherent maternal-fetal scene, not an isolated floating placenta. |
| 5. Ovary and follicular fluid | Separate recognizable ovary, then a follicular magnification. | Human follicular-fluid study comes first: microplastics detected in 14 of 18 samples around developing eggs. Mouse/cell work can be secondary context. | Do not represent an ovary as a generic elongated blob or merge it visually with the pregnancy scene. |
| 6. Testicular tissue | Accurate male pelvic or isolated testicular anatomy with labels. | Lead with what was found in the human samples, then explain why researchers are studying reproductive health. | Avoid methods-first jargon. The structure must be identifiable to a non-scientist. |
| 7. Exit to action | Return from anatomy to clear action and deeper science. | Make the next route clear: Solutions, Science, or the book. | The end does not feel like a disconnected static section. |

### Anatomy behavior

- Scroll drives the primary story.
- Organ/chapter navigation is also clickable, keyboard usable, and touch usable.
- Scroll state, highlighted label, shown structure, and finding card always stay synchronized.
- Do not make hover the only interaction.
- Reduced-motion mode provides the exact same information in stable visual panels without camera movement.
- Use real, licensed, anatomically recognizable source models or reviewed clinical illustration. Do not fabricate anatomy with decorative primitives.
- Every chapter includes: a structure caption, main finding, a concise meaning statement, expandable study context, and direct primary-study link.

### Exposure routes and action bridge

- Replace ambiguous “routes repeat” and “repetition principle” copy.
- Show six clear public routes: **air, water, food, heat and food contact, fast fashion / synthetic textiles, skincare and cosmetics**.
- Each route receives a small representative visual or a coherent editorial visual treatment. It can lead to deeper content if the destination exists.
- Add fast fashion and skincare/cosmetics everywhere the site speaks about major exposure pathways. They were explicitly identified as currently neglected.
- The home action preview should be brief and point to Solutions. It should not duplicate a full guide on the homepage.
- Use the Quick Action Card’s three core rules as the primary compact action message: do not heat plastic, do not store food in plastic, do not drink from plastic.

### Home sections after anatomy

- Present solutions/action, then the book, then Dr. Haddad, Events & Media, and community/newsletter in a deliberate sequence.
- Keep the lower-page “greatest inheritance” quote only where it earns its space. It may appear near the lower site/footer zone; do not repeat it randomly.
- Use the real approved portrait and TEDx visual. Do not substitute a generic physician or unrelated media image.

## 4. Science system

| Evidence item | Required treatment | Source / decision |
|---|---|---|
| Brain | Keep Nature Medicine human-tissue finding. Translate result first, then provide study context. | Primary paper linked. |
| Carotid plaque | Preserve as a 2024 NEJM study, distinct from the new coronary-circulation study. | Primary paper linked. |
| Acute heart attack / coronary blood | Add the 2025 European Heart Journal study. Say what the study found in its exact groups and do not convert association into proof of causation. | Supplied source link. |
| Placenta | Keep the maternal and fetal-facing context and original source. | Primary paper linked. |
| Testicular tissue | Explain consumer meaning first. Do not use a technical headline about relationships and tissue measures. | Confirm exact wording against primary study. |
| Follicular fluid / eggs | Human study must lead. State 14 of 18 samples and the reported concentration; mouse/cell research is secondary. Explain “follicular fluid” in everyday language. | Supplied PubMed source. |
| Public-health sources | Keep reputable primary/regulatory material where it is useful. Remove repetitive source blocks that do not add a new purpose. | WHO, FDA, MD Anderson, and comparable reputable organizations. |

### Science writing rules

- Lead with the finding the reader came to understand.
- Explain technical words in the sentence where they first matter.
- Keep one short limitation/context line where it changes the meaning. Do not replace the entire reader experience with “what we do not know.”
- Remove generic “we take uncertainty seriously,” “how we verify,” and similar meta-writing from primary reading paths.
- Do not make a specific illness, fertility, detoxification, or treatment claim unless Dr. Haddad has approved the wording and the cited evidence supports that claim.

## 5. Solutions, Quick Action Card, and guide system

### Required public resources

- Recreate the supplied **Quick Action Card** as a downloadable, branded resource and a readable web version.
- Preserve the 12 actions as Dr. Haddad’s authored action card. For any individual health/“detox” statement, label the source appropriately and obtain his final approval before turning it into a standalone medical promise.
- Give visitors a clear short action plan, then detailed guides.

### Required guide topics and practical direction

| Topic | Required plain-language direction from transcript | Publication condition |
|---|---|---|
| Drinking water | Avoid plastic water bottles. Explain filtration simply, including reverse osmosis where appropriate. | Final claim and product guidance review. |
| Food heating and storage | Do not microwave or heat food in plastic. Use glass, ceramic, or stainless steel. Do not tell visitors to cool food before moving it into plastic storage. | Final clinical/editorial approval. |
| Kitchen conversion | Replace plastic kitchen tools, plates, cups, and food-storage supplies with wood, glass, ceramic, or stainless steel as practical. | Needs clear scope so it stays useful rather than wasteful. |
| Single-use plastic | Add a direct, memorable message about eliminating disposable plastic cups, plates, and foodware where possible. | Build into action guidance and future merch/affiliate context. |
| Canned and packaged foods | Include Dr. Haddad’s requested concern in a source-backed, precisely worded guide. | Requires approved claim language and citations. |
| Indoor dust / air | Cover ventilation, cleaning, and practical dust reduction. | Source-backed guide. |
| Fast fashion | Add a dedicated exposure and action section, including synthetic textiles and replacement choices. | Required across home, solutions, guides, and future affiliate categories. |
| Skincare and cosmetics | Add a dedicated exposure and action section, including how to choose cleaner products. | Required across home, solutions, guides, and future affiliate categories. |

### Solutions UX rules

- No “do not turn precaution into panic,” “frequency × contact × heat,” or generic self-correcting copy as a major section.
- Instructions should be direct enough for a non-scientist to act without reading a lecture first.
- The site must still distinguish education from individual medical advice in the appropriate legal/support locations, not by interrupting every action card.

## 6. The book and ecommerce

### Book experience

- Maintain the requested premium scroll/read-map journey. Do not remove it just because it is more difficult to implement.
- Correct the book asset to the supplied published cover showing the collaborator credit.
- Remove redundant book-introduction blocks and any stray “Homo Plasticus / Plasticus” duplicate copy.
- Desktop composition: book/cover/open spread stays on the left; title, premise, format, price, delivery note, CTA, and related reading information stay on the right. Nothing may cross the center divider or overlap.
- The sequence can move from cover to a purposeful open spread/page-turn experience. It must use approved book themes/sample material, not invented filler.
- Reduced-motion version shows a stable cover/open-spread alternative.
- Book page needs a clear FAQ: digital format, audience, what is and is not medical advice, delivery, technical support, and final-sale policy.

### Checkout and fulfillment: operational requirements

1. Preserve the current WooCommerce/WooPayments purchase route unless Dr. Haddad explicitly decides to replace the payment platform. Existing payment methods should remain available only if they are truly configured on the live merchant account.
2. Configure the ebook as a secure digital product with the final file, order access rules, purchase receipt, download availability, and technical-support route.
3. The buyer must be able to download the ebook after successful purchase. Email-only access is not acceptable as the sole delivery method.
4. Provide download access on the confirmation page **and** in the order email, with a controlled re-download/support process for genuine access issues.
5. Test at least: successful card/express purchase, failed payment, duplicate click, expired/reused download link behavior, order receipt, mobile checkout, refund-policy link, and support escalation.
6. Do not collect or handle payment cards in the Say No to Plastic application. Payment stays with the configured PCI-compliant checkout provider.
7. A future native checkout or Stripe Link-style flow is a separate, explicit platform decision. It requires a real merchant account, tax settings, payment compliance, refund behavior, and order fulfillment integration. It is not a visual popup task.

### Ecommerce inputs required from Dr. Haddad / owner

- Final file(s): PDF, EPUB, or both; file size; download name; page count; publication date; price; tax and sales-country policy.
- WooCommerce/WordPress owner or administrator access, or an agreed handoff for the owner to configure each required product setting.
- Confirmed support email and order-support owner.
- Final published-book credit wording.

## 9. Contact, legal, affiliate, and trust operations

### Contact

- Lead with “Ask a question” or equally approachable language.
- Support categories: ebook access, media/speaking, research, partnerships, and general questions.
- Keep a separate contact route even if media inquiries are also promoted from Community.
- Do not make a large “this project cannot provide medical advice” statement the dominant contact-page experience. Keep medical-boundary language accurate and placed appropriately.

### Legal and policy

- Privacy policy must match the actual analytics, email provider, checkout provider, and data practices before launch.
- Ebook policy: digital sales are final, but genuine technical download/access problems receive support.
- Affiliate disclosure must be visible near any affiliate link and on a dedicated policy page.
- Medical disclaimer explains educational use and lack of individual diagnosis/treatment without becoming the site’s dominant marketing voice.
- Terms, refund policy, support address, and media-contact details must be cross-linked and current.

### Affiliate system

- Do not publish empty affiliate shelves or invented recommendations.
- Create a merchant/product registry before links go live: product, category, material claim, evidence/source, retailer, commission relationship, disclosure copy, price-check date, image rights, and link destination.
- First categories discussed: plastic-free clothing such as hemp/bamboo/cotton, kitchen materials, water filtration, skincare/cosmetics, and other lower-plastic household goods.
- Future affiliate pages must show real product review criteria and customer-relevant tradeoffs, not vague “plastic-free” marketing.

### Gate 3: page-by-page completion
