THE ENDOCRINE & METABOLIC SYSTEM
The Body’s Chemical Messengers
Your endocrine system is a network of glands that produces hormones—the chemical
messengers that regulate nearly every function in your body. From growth and metabolism to
reproduction, sleep, mood, and energy, hormones help keep your body in balance.

Because hormones operate in incredibly small amounts, even subtle disruptions have the
potential to influence normal biological function. This is why scientists have become increasingly
interested in studying how certain environmental chemicals, including some associated with
plastics, may interact with the endocrine system.




Why the Endocrine System Matters
Unlike the heart or lungs, the endocrine system doesn’t consist of a single organ. It includes the
thyroid, pancreas, adrenal glands, ovaries, testes, and other hormone-producing tissues that
work together to coordinate the body’s most important functions.

Many plastics contain or are manufactured with chemicals such as bisphenols (including
BPA) and phthalates, which are known as endocrine-disrupting chemicals because they can
interfere with normal hormone signaling.

It is important to distinguish these chemicals from microplastics themselves. While researchers
are studying the effects of microscopic plastic particles, endocrine-disrupting chemicals have
been investigated for decades and have a much larger body of scientific evidence.




What the Research Has Found
Large human studies have linked exposure to certain endocrine-disrupting chemicals with
changes in hormone levels, reduced fertility, earlier puberty, metabolic disorders, obesity, and
type 2 diabetes. While these studies do not always prove cause and effect, the consistency of
the findings has raised significant public health concerns.

Researchers are now investigating whether microplastics may also contribute to these effects by
carrying endocrine-disrupting chemicals into the body or by triggering inflammation and
oxidative stress. This area of research is still developing, and many questions remain
unanswered.

Scientists are also studying whether long-term exposure to these chemicals may influence
thyroid function, insulin regulation, testosterone, estrogen, and other hormones that play
essential roles throughout life.




What This Means
Hormones work like an orchestra. Each one must be released at the right time, in the right
amount, to keep the body functioning properly.

Scientists have shown that certain chemicals associated with plastics can interfere with this
delicate communication system. Exactly how much everyday exposure contributes to long-term
health risks is still being investigated, but reducing unnecessary exposure has become an
important focus of environmental health research.

Understanding the endocrine system reminds us that our bodies are influenced not only by the
food we eat and the exercise we perform, but also by the environment we live in every day.




Key Takeaways
   ●​ The endocrine system regulates growth, metabolism, reproduction, sleep, mood, and
      many other essential body functions.
   ●​ Certain chemicals associated with plastics, including BPA and phthalates, are
      recognized endocrine disruptors and have been extensively studied.
   ●​ Human studies have linked higher exposure to some endocrine-disrupting chemicals
      with changes in hormone function, fertility, metabolism, and other health outcomes.
   ●​ Researchers are now investigating whether microplastics themselves may also influence
      the endocrine system, but this research is still evolving.
   ●​ More studies are needed to fully understand how long-term exposure to both plastic
      particles and endocrine-disrupting chemicals may affect human health over a lifetime.
