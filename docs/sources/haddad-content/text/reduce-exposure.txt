HOW TO REDUCE YOUR EXPOSURE
Small Changes Can Make a Big Difference
By now, you’ve seen that microplastics have become part of our modern environment. They
have been detected in the air we breathe, the water we drink, the food we eat, and in many
human tissues.

The good news is that this isn’t a story about perfection.

It’s a story about awareness.

Completely avoiding plastic is neither realistic nor necessary. Instead, the goal is to identify the
biggest sources of exposure and make simple, practical changes that can meaningfully reduce
your overall lifetime exposure.

Think of it as improving your environment one decision at a time.




Focus on the Biggest Sources First
Not all exposures are equal.

Rather than worrying about every piece of plastic you encounter, focus on the habits that may
have the greatest impact.

Replace your plastic kitchen supplies with wood, ceramic, stainless steel, or glass

Avoid plastic water bottles.

Install a reverse osmosis water filter in your kitchen

Avoid heating food in plastic containers.

Store leftovers in glass or stainless steel instead of plastic

Eliminate your use of single-use plastics

When possible, choose fresh or minimally processed foods over heavily packaged alternatives.

These small changes are easy to implement and can become lifelong habits. None of them
need to happen overnight. Progress is built one habit at a time.
Think Beyond Food
Our environment extends beyond the kitchen.

Synthetic clothing, household dust, cosmetics, personal care products, and indoor air all
contribute to our overall environmental exposure.

Whenever practical:

• Choose natural fibers such as cotton, linen, or wool.

• Vacuum and dust regularly using a HEPA filter when possible.

• Ventilate your home by opening windows when outdoor air quality allows.

• Choose personal care products that are free of unnecessary plastic ingredients and
endocrine-disrupting chemicals.

Again, perfection isn’t the goal. Awareness is, because awareness is the first step towards
meaningful action.




Every Choice Matters
One person changing one habit may seem insignificant.

But millions of people making small, informed decisions can create meaningful change, for their
own health and for the environment.

The choices we make today influence not only our own wellbeing, but also the world future
generations will inherit.

That is why education matters. That is why awareness matters. And that is why this movement
exists.




Key Takeaways
   ●​ You do not need to eliminate plastic from your life to make a difference.
   ●​ Focus first on the biggest and most practical sources of exposure.
   ●​ Small, consistent changes often have a greater impact than dramatic, short-lived efforts.
   ●​ Improving your environment is an investment in your long-term health.
   ●​ Awareness is the first step toward meaningful change.
