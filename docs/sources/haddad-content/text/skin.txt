THE SKIN
Our Largest Organ and Our First Line of Defense
Your skin is the largest organ in your body. It serves as a protective barrier between you and the
outside world, shielding you from injury, infection, ultraviolet radiation, and countless
environmental exposures every day.

Because our skin is in constant contact with clothing, personal care products, cosmetics, and
the surrounding environment, researchers are investigating whether it may also be exposed to
microscopic plastic particles.




Why the Skin Matters
Healthy skin is remarkably effective at protecting the body.

The outermost layer, known as the skin barrier, prevents most unwanted substances from
entering while helping retain moisture and protecting against infection.

Scientists generally believe that intact, healthy skin is an excellent barrier against most
microplastics. However, researchers are studying whether extremely small nanoplastics or
damaged skin may allow greater interaction with underlying tissues.

In addition, many skincare and cosmetic products are packaged in plastic containers or may
contain synthetic polymers that come into direct contact with the skin.




What the Research Has Found
Unlike the lungs or digestive system, the skin is not considered a major route through which
microplastics enter the body.

Current research suggests that most microplastic particles are too large to penetrate healthy
skin. However, scientists continue to investigate whether nanoplastics—the smallest
particles—may behave differently, particularly in damaged or diseased skin.

Researchers are also studying whether plastic particles that settle on the skin contribute to local
inflammation or oxidative stress, although human evidence remains limited.
More importantly, many scientists have focused on chemicals associated with plastics, including
certain ingredients used in cosmetics and personal care products. Some of these chemicals
have been shown to enter the body through the skin and are being studied for their potential
effects on hormone function and overall health.




What This Means
Your skin is an incredibly effective natural barrier, and current evidence suggests it protects you
from most microplastic particles.

At the same time, your skin represents one of your body’s closest interactions with the
environment. Understanding what comes into contact with it—including the products we use
every day—remains an important part of environmental health.

As research continues, scientists hope to better understand whether prolonged exposure to
nanoplastics or certain plastic-associated chemicals has any meaningful effect on skin health or
overall wellbeing.




Key Takeaways
   ●​ The skin is the body’s largest organ and its first line of defense against the environment.
   ●​ Healthy skin appears to block most microplastic particles from entering the body.
   ●​ Scientists are investigating whether extremely small nanoplastics behave differently,
      particularly when the skin barrier is damaged.
   ●​ Researchers are also studying chemicals associated with plastics that may be absorbed
      through the skin from cosmetics and personal care products.
   ●​ Current evidence suggests that inhalation and ingestion remain the primary routes of
      exposure, while skin exposure continues to be an area of ongoing research.
